README rewrite: the front door for our own software

Status:; active goal since 2026-11-13

  • Status: planned
  • Persona: Diego (primary reader -- installs, configures, watches it run); Ingrid (reads it for packaging truth); Samira (holds its claims to evidence)
  • Serves: every new visitor to the repository, and sdanfa's class of user who arrives with one feature in mind
  • Scope: the udpspeeder-simd component's two READMEs and the marks guide, with new mermaid diagrams and an elaborated SIMD section; no code, no release

Why

The READMEs are still upstream's: their structure, their diagrams, their story. The software underneath stopped being upstream long ago -- the SIMD kernels, the DNS lease manager that follows a moving endpoint, the socket marks with their honor-or-refuse contract, four-host CI that reads the priority byte off the wire, the OpenWrt packages, the renamed binary. A visitor reading the front door meets wangyu-'s UDPspeeder with footnotes, not the software they would actually run. The Chinese README compounds it: a translation of the old English one, one version behind even that.

The rewrite keeps three things deliberately. Full, prominent credit to wangyu-/UDPspeeder -- this is a fork and its foundation is honest work, MIT carried forward. The two READMEs stay 1:1 aligned, English and Chinese carrying the same structure, section for section, so neither language is a second-class reader again. And the diagrams are mermaid in the markdown itself, so they render wherever the README does and version with the text that explains them.

What the new front door says

  • What this is: a fork of UDPspeeder that became its own tunnel -- the one paragraph lineage with the credit link up front, not buried in a footer.
  • Why it is fast: the SIMD story elaborated from OPTIMIZATION.md -- the vectorized FEC kernels, the per-CPU dispatch tiers (SSSE3, AVX2, NEON, and the PowerPC SPE path), what the dispatcher picks at runtime and the measured gain per tier -- with a mermaid flowchart of the dispatch.
  • Why it is different: the endpoint that follows DNS, the socket marks, the platforms and binaries we actually ship.
  • VPN over the tunnel is a key goal: an applied family with one backbone, the MTU ledger (path MTU x inner VPN -> safe inner MTU and the knob that sets it, every number verified by capturing real stacked packets) -- WireGuard at full MTU with mark coordination, OpenVPN TCP carried on UDP (the loss-rescue claim with its double-spend tension left live), the stranger scenarios (raw TCP, IPv6 inner, double stacking, the no-VPN case), and the anti-loop route exception generalized.
  • The applied sections: tuning a lossy link (ratio table, live fifo levers, measure-your-own-link), the moving endpoint, what this is not (five honest lines), which binary for which CPU, and troubleshooting routed to its own guide.
  • Quick start both ways: command line and OpenWrt UCI, every applied section ending in the two-door pattern (CLI block and UCI stanza side by side).
  • New mermaid diagrams: the tunnel architecture with the marks drawn on the outer packets, the endpoint re-pointing when DNS moves, and the SIMD dispatch -- three blocks in the markdown, no image files.
  • The evidence posture, in plain words: what we claim, we test; the wire proofs described as they run.
  • Platform table, privileges table, the feed, the license story.

Writing charter

Four sources govern the prose and the ink, and every task's verify line enforces them. Practitioner Voice (Highsmith on martinfowler.com): sections open with claims, authority comes from consequence, scars are credibility, tensions are named and left live, compression respects the reader, a carry phrase closes. Tufte: data-ink maximized, tables as small multiples, graphics inside the prose flow, every number adjacent to its origin, empty cells stated as data. Diataxis: every section declares one quadrant and keeps its register; the README routes what it does not own; blur is the named failure. The Good Docs Project: status and engage sections present; an EN-zh terminology table keeps the two READMEs a translation of record. Personas describe readers inside this host's rooms only; the shipped documents never name them, and no methodology vocabulary appears in the artifacts.
